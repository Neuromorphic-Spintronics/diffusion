\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Many computational-physics problems require ensembles of trajectories. Thermal
fluctuations, unresolved variables, uncertain initial conditions, and noisy forcing
make future states random. When a stochastic differential equation (SDE) is known,
numerical integration can generate the ensemble \cite{kloeden1992numerical}. When
only measured trajectories are available, direct simulation is not possible.
Repeated simulation can also be costly when the physical parameters vary.

A surrogate for this task must model the conditional distribution of a full
trajectory. It must preserve temporal correlations, marginal distributions, and
dependence on the experimental inputs. Pointwise squared-error regression usually
estimates a conditional mean and does not represent stochastic spread.
Physics-informed neural networks and neural operators learn equation-constrained
solutions or maps between functions \cite{raissi2019pinn,li2021fno,
lu2021deeponet}. In their standard forms, they do not learn conditional
distributions over complete stochastic paths.

A diffusion model adds Gaussian noise to a trajectory and trains a neural network
to predict that noise \cite{sohldickstein2015,ho2020ddpm}. Under the
mean-squared-error objective, the noise predictor also defines a score estimate for
the noisy data distribution \cite{vincent2011connection,song2021sde}.
Conditioning on physical parameters or driving signals selects the trajectory
distribution. Denoising diffusion implicit models (DDIMs) use the same denoiser
with fewer reverse evaluations \cite{song2021ddim}. With $\eta=0$ and fixed
initial noise, the DDIM update is deterministic. This property permits
derivative-based inverse estimation.

This paper presents \texttt{diffusionsde}, a package for one-dimensional time
series. The call \texttt{DDPM()} constructs the model. The \texttt{fit} method
uses trajectory--condition pairs, and \texttt{generate} samples trajectories for
supplied conditions. The minimal example uses scalar data and the default
trajectory length. \texttt{DiffusionConfig} controls the trajectory length,
channel counts, noise schedule, and network settings.

\begin{pythonlisting}
from diffusionsde import DDPM

model = DDPM()
model.fit(train_dataset)
samples = model.generate(conditioning, n_samples=64)
\end{pythonlisting}

Sections~\ref{sec:physical_problem} and \ref{sec:conditional_diffusion} define the
data representation, the training objective evaluated by \texttt{fit}, and the
reverse processes used by \texttt{generate}. Section~\ref{sec:examples} gives the
numerical verification cases. The experimental systems then use the same
representation and interface.
